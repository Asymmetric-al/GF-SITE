# Audience Profile: Local Church Planters and Indigenous Ministry Leaders

Local church planters are **Global Fellowship's primary human anchor**, never secondary beneficiaries[^source-synthesis]. Most Global Fellowship workers come from the regions where they serve. Communication directed to them must speak peer-to-peer, translate cleanly across languages, and respect their leadership from the first sentence.

## Who They Are & What They Carry

- Pastors, evangelists, and indigenous ministry founders already serving in communities with little or no Gospel access across South Asia, Southeast Asia, Central Asia, East Asia, Europe, the Middle East, North Africa, and the Americas.
- Carrying real spiritual, social, financial, and sometimes legal pressure (for example, local property pressure or tightened restrictions on unregistered congregations), often feeling isolated from a wider trusted family.

## Real Questions & Hesitations They Bring

1. _"If we join an international fellowship, will headquarters take over our name, force Western church-planting methods on us, or treat us like employees?"_
2. _"Will Western visitors or stories expose our families and congregations to local officials or social backlash?"_
3. _"When ministry is slow, painful, or lonely, will anyone stay beside us as family, or does care disappear if numbers dip?"_
4. _"Do we have real authority to edit our own stories, decline a visiting team, or stay visibly unbranded where foreign-NGO laws make association dangerous?"_

## What Must Be Clear Immediately

- **Real authority stays local:** `Global coordinates. Regional contextualizes. Local leads.` Methods, timing, team structure, and ministry identity stay in local hands (`integration without assimilation`).
- **Editorial veto & security sovereignty:** Local leaders choose what is shared, hold final approval over stories and translations, may decline photos or visits, and may use either the local-first endorsement lockup (`In fellowship with Global Fellowship`) or **zero public attribution** where law or safety requires it[^brand-architecture].
- **Relationship precedes formal partnership:** Onboarding begins with direct relationship building and an in-person regional visit before any formal acceptance[^source-synthesis].
- **Reciprocal dignity:** Local leaders are teachers, hosts, and peers from whom Western and global churches come to learn.
