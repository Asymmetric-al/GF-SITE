# Global Fellowship Brand Character and Values in Action

What people experience externally must match how people are treated internally. Global Fellowship's character comes directly from its seven documented core values and everyday operating culture, not marketing adjectives[^source-synthesis].

## 1. Five Enduring Character Traits

1. **Convicted and Humble:** Direct about Jesus Christ, Scripture, repentance, baptism, and local church formation, yet quick to pray first, listen across cultures, and hold methods with open hands[^foundations][^source-synthesis].
2. **Warm Through Practical Attention:** Shown by remembering names, keeping promises, replying to letters, checking on a single worker during a quiet season, and giving clear answers rather than cheerful slogans[^source-synthesis].
3. **Purposeful Yet Patient:** Urgent about unreached communities with little or no Gospel access (Joshua Project research context tracked under `OVR-19`), yet patient with the years required to learn a language and grow healthy local elders[^source-synthesis].
4. **Candid About Cost and Health:** Honest about loneliness, spiritual opposition, grief, financial boundaries, and organizational limits. Celebrates faithful rest as readily as visible fruit.
5. **Inviting Without Pressure:** Guided by Global Fellowship's documented standard of _"relational warmth, sound judgment, and persistence without pressure"_[^source-synthesis].

## 2. Seven Core Values in Communication & Design

| Core Value | Required Behavior | Explicitly Forbidden |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Gospel first, infrastructure follows** | Explain buildings, businesses, training, and projects strictly in relation to discipleship and local churches. Keep branding practical and affordable. | Making a building, app, or brand refresh the hero of the story. |
| **2. Relationships are a priority** | Build genuine two-way connection (Bridge Project letters, listening-first trips, personal replies). Include spouses, children, and single workers equally. | Treating supporters as transaction records or picturing "family" as exclusively married parents. |
| **3. Local leadership** | Give local leaders opening voice, editorial veto, right to decline visits or photos, and top billing on co-branded pieces. | Publishing a story about a local ministry without the leader's review or implying Western control. |
| **4. Humility** | Credit God for spiritual fruit and honor local hosts, peer churches, and regional caregivers plainly. | Triumphalist statistics, "only we do this" claims, or disparaging peer ministries. |
| **5. Health above results** | Support quiet/rest-month updates (plus a valid No-Send Option). Guard personal privacy in member care. | Pressuring workers for monthly miracle reports or turning personal struggle into fundraising drama. |
| **6. Biblical principle over policy** | Measure methods and stories by Scripture, backed by clear safeguarding, privacy, consent, and financial policies. | Repeating the retired legacy claim (`no written policies`) contradicted by safeguarding standards. |
| **7. Trust and transparency** | Date every figure, footnote scope limits, share annual statements openly, explain ECFA plainly, and hold unconfirmed figures until verified. | Hiding consolidated accounting complexity, quoting undated numbers, or exaggerating overhead claims. |

## 3. What "Family" Means (And Does Not Mean)

- **Spiritually:** Belonging to one household in Christ (Ephesians 2:19) sharing prayer, candor, and mutual burden-bearing across cultures[^source-synthesis].
- **Literally:** Guarding home life, marriage, children, teenagers, and emotional endurance while keeping **single workers** explicitly visible and cared for.
- **Boundary:** "Family" never excuses informal safeguarding, vague finances, or missing boundaries. Workers always retain safe escalation routes outside a direct supervisor's line[^source-synthesis].
