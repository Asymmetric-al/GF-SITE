# Branding Frameworks and Creative Craft References

This document records the branding, communication, and visual craft principles applied to Global Fellowship's brand system, stating honestly what source materials were consulted and where commercial frameworks were adapted to protect Christian theology and human dignity.

## Scope of Consultation (Honesty Disclosure)

In keeping with strict factual accuracy, this research consulted publicly available official excerpts, author summaries, and published methodology documentation for the four anchor texts below rather than complete full-book manuscripts[^miller-framework][^brand-idea][^durham-framework][^heath-stick]. Every principle applied was vetted against Global Fellowship's theology, protected foundations, and primary sources[^foundations].

## 1. StoryBrand 2.0 (Donald Miller) and Its Necessary Ethical Adaptation

**What StoryBrand contributes:** Clarity under cognitive load. Visitors quickly abandon vague, insider-heavy organizations that talk mainly about internal structure. StoryBrand's 7-part structure (Character, Problem [external, internal, philosophical], Guide [empathy + authority], Plan, Call to Action [direct + transitional], Avoiding Failure, Achieving Success) disciplines every communication piece to answer plainly: what is at stake, what is the path, and what is the clear next step[^miller-framework].

**Critical ethical adaptation for Global Fellowship:** Commercial StoryBrand puts the customer or donor at the center as the "hero" and the organization as the "guide." Applied uncritically to Christian missions, that formula creates **donor saviorism**, turning Western givers into heroic rescuers and local believers into passive props. Global Fellowship rejects that inversion:

- **God and Christ's redeeming mission remain central.** Neither Global Fellowship nor any giver saves anyone; God builds his church.
- **Local church planters retain full agency, leadership, and voice.** They are co-laborers, pastors, and teachers in the story, never helpless beneficiaries waiting for Western rescue.
- **Prospective participants (churches, givers, prayer partners, candidates) are invited as humble members of a shared family.** For each audience's specific decision journey, StoryBrand's clarity tools still serve powerfully: name their real tension, meet them with warmth and competence, offer a clear 3-step path, provide both a direct CTA and a low-friction transitional invitation (praying, reading, or joining The Bridge Project), and describe faithfully what partnership makes possible without guaranteeing spiritual outcomes.

## 2. The Brand IDEA (Nathalie Laidler-Kylander and Julia Shepard Stenzel)

From the Stanford Social Innovation Review excerpt of _The Brand IDEA_, strong nonprofit brands operate differently from top-down corporate brands through three linked principles[^brand-idea]:

- **Brand Integrity:** Internal culture and external expression must match. Global Fellowship's brand cannot feel slick, expensive, or triumphalist when its real culture is lean (~$251.6k 2025 Home Office operating budget), relational, humble, and candid about hardship.
- **Brand Democracy:** Empower staff, regional leaders, church planters, and sending churches to live and express the brand authentically rather than policing headquarters-only scripts. This harmonizes directly with `"Global coordinates. Regional contextualizes. Local leads."`
- **Brand Affinity:** Build long-term partnerships and shared ownership rather than zero-sum competition with peer ministries or local church identities. This reinforces `"Integration without assimilation"` and forbids disparaging peer agencies.

## 3. Brandraising (Sarah Durham)

Big Duck's _Brandraising_ model organizes nonprofit communication into a three-tier pyramid where every layer depends on the one beneath it[^durham-framework]:

1. **Organizational level (foundation):** Mission, vision, values, strategy, and real operating commitments (permanently anchored by [Protected Foundations](../brand/foundations.md) and Global Fellowship's 7 values).
2. **Identity level (translation):** Positioning, personality, messaging architecture, verbal voice, and visual identity that translate organizational truth into clear, recognizable form.
3. **Experiential level (encounter):** Every concrete touchpoint where people meet Global Fellowship (prayer cards, worker letters, church kits, annual reports, emails, Bridge Project letters, Faith on the Frontlines invites, presentations, and the future website).

_Brandraising_ warns against polishing experiential surfaces while leaving organizational messaging vague or misaligned across teams.

## 4. Made to Stick (Chip and Dan Heath)

The six stickiness principles sharpen whether Global Fellowship's discretionary copy and explanations actually stay with an audience without gimmicks[^heath-stick]:

- **Simple:** Lead with one unmistakable core idea before unpacking nuance.
- **Unexpected:** Break category expectations truthfully (an international missions fellowship whose correspondence program asks for zero money, and whose cross-cultural missionaries serve under local leaders).
- **Concrete:** Ground missiological truths in physical, human-scale realities (a neighbor's kitchen table, a river baptism, a handwritten letter, or Scripture read in a mother tongue) rather than abstractions.
- **Credible:** Use dated, transparent numbers, visible footnotes, and local voices rather than inflated claims.
- **Emotional:** Evoke authentic care, reverence, friendship, and solidarity rather than guilt manipulation or poverty imagery.
- **Stories:** Let real, authorized accounts carry theological weight and relational truth.

## 5. Cross-Sector Creative Craft Benchmarks

To avoid generic nonprofit templates while staying deeply appropriate to a lean Christian fellowship, visual and editorial craft draws structural lessons (never copying signature looks) from disciplined benchmarks:

- **Editorial bookmaking and field journals:** Generous white space, disciplined typographic hierarchy, serif/sans pairing engineered for reading comfort across print and modest mobile phones, and restrained accent color used with meaning rather than decoration.
- **charity: water and International Justice Mission (IJM):** Transparent explanation of financial mechanics and sober, dignity-first storytelling about serious realities without sensationalism or pity.
- **BibleProject and Lausanne Movement:** Theological seriousness paired with warm, approachable clarity that respects both seasoned pastors and curious newcomers.
- **Anti-cliché visual guardrails:** Avoid monochrome warm-only cream-and-terracotta lifestyle templates lacking architectural counterweight (Living Nearness anchors Warm Paper and Kiln Clay against deep Harbor Teal and Harbor Night) and avoid dark neon dashboards, green-primary or saffron-orange political color collisions, monospace dossier aesthetics on restricted materials, and worn missions tropes (globes pierced by crosses, hands circling the earth, map pins).
