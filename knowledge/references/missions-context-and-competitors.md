# Contemporary Missions Context and Sector Landscape

Effective communication about Global Fellowship requires accurate understanding of global Gospel access, precision with missiological definitions, and fair, respectful awareness of peer ministries.

## The Current State of Global Gospel Access

Always cite the source, reporting date, and statistical definition whenever using global missions figures. Never blend definitions across Joshua Project, Gordon-Conwell, and Lausanne, because each measures a different unit (people groups, global religious demographics, and strategic movement trends).

- **Global Christian demographics (Gordon-Conwell CSGC, _Status of Global Christianity_ series, consulted Nov 2026; research-only figures pending exact edition/table pinning under `OVR-19`):** Multi-decade demographic research shows a sustained shift placing the clear majority of global Christians in the Global South (Africa, Asia, Latin America, and Oceania) alongside North America and Europe. In ready-to-use public copy, describe this Global South shift qualitatively; keep approximate series figures (roughly 2.67 billion Christians out of roughly 8.2 billion people worldwide, ~32%) strictly as internal research context until the exact current annual CSGC edition year and table are pinned under `OVR-19`[^gordon-conwell].
- **Unreached People Groups (Joshua Project live tracker, consulted Nov 2026; exact publish snapshot date to be pinned prior to external publication under `OVR-19`):** Joshua Project tracks roughly 17,300 people groups worldwide; roughly 7,200 of those groups (about 42% of people groups), representing roughly 3.6 billion people (about 43.5% of world population), are classified as **unreached**. Because Joshua Project updates continuously, always pin the exact live dashboard date immediately before external print or web publication[^joshua-project].
- **Exact definition of "unreached" (UPG):** Under Joshua Project's published quantitative threshold, a people group is classified as unreached when **2% or fewer** are evangelical Christians **and** **5% or fewer** are Christian adherents overall[^joshua-project]. Missiologists commonly treat that threshold as an indicator that an indigenous believing community typically lacks the numbers and resources to evangelize the wider people group without cross-cultural or near-culture help (treat as a research heuristic alongside local pastors' firsthand knowledge, not an absolute claim about any individual community).
- **Unengaged, Unreached People Groups (UUPGs):** Under Joshua Project's standard definition, a narrower, continuously updated subset of UPGs characterized by the **absence of known active efforts to establish self-sustaining churches** consistent with evangelical faith and practice (do not quote a fixed UUPG count without pinning the live Joshua Project snapshot date under `OVR-19`)[^joshua-project].
- **Urbanization, migration, and growing headcount (Lausanne _State of the Great Commission_ 2024 Seoul report series):** The absolute number of people without meaningful Gospel access has increased over recent decades because global population growth is concentrated in communities with the smallest Christian presence, alongside accelerating urbanization, migration, diaspora formation, and post-Christian secularization. For Global Fellowship's storytelling and design, this means representing **contemporary urban apartment fellowships and near-culture diaspora church planting** alongside rural village congregations rather than defaulting solely to rural imagery[^lausanne-sotgc].
- **Polycentric missions ("from everywhere to everyone"):** Majority World churches are primary senders and theological leaders in the contemporary Great Commission, calling for mission partnerships structured around mutual belonging rather than one-way Western control[^lausanne-sotgc][^gordon-conwell].

### Statistical Hygiene Rule on Popular Missions Heuristics

Many agencies repeat the slogan `` `only 3% of missionaries and 1% of Christian giving go to the unreached` `` (or shorter `` `3% / 1%` `` heuristics). Primary missiological critiques document that those heuristics rely on uneven agency surveys and routinely undercount indigenous, near-culture, and bi-vocational Majority World church planters. Global Fellowship must **never** repeat unqualified `` `3% / 1%` `` slogans as undisputed global fact; see [Retired Legacy Claims](../messaging/retired-claims.md).

## Sector Clichés and Blind Spots to Leave Behind

1. **Interchangeable category wallpaper:** Phrases like `"reaching the unreached,"` `"transforming nations,"` `"impacting lives,"` and `"your gift changes everything"` can be swapped across twenty agencies without changing a word.
2. **Donor-as-savior framing:** Treating Western givers as heroic rescuers and local believers as passive recipients or cost-effective labor (`` `"for just $X/month, a native missionary works 10x cheaper"` ``) violates biblical dignity and contradicts Global Fellowship's theology of the body of Christ.
3. **Heroic Western pioneer mythology:** Equating "missionary" with a Westerner boarding a plane erases the majority of frontline church planters who already speak the language and carry the cost at home.
4. **Metrics without soul, or sentiment without honesty:** Inflated guaranteed-outcome claims erode trust among careful pastors; vague warmth without dated proof leaves audiences unsure whether anything real is happening.
5. **Visual clichés:** Globes pierced by crosses, smiling hands encircling a planet, desaturated stock portraits of children, and loud charity templates.

## Global Fellowship's Specific, Truthful Distinction

Global Fellowship never manufactures exclusivity by pretending other ministries do not care about local churches. Its credible distinction is the **specific combination** rooted in Don and Kathy Oates's Liberia service under a local pastor before founding Global Fellowship in Auburn, California in 1989 and its operating model[^source-synthesis]:

- **One shared family where local leaders still lead when cross-cultural workers arrive:** Unlike traditional Western sending agencies centered on outsiders and unlike donor-to-native funding conduits that keep indigenous ministries at arm's length, Global Fellowship brings near-culture church planters and cross-cultural missionaries into **one worldwide fellowship** where cross-cultural workers serve alongside and under local leadership.
- **Integration without assimilation:** Local ministries keep their indigenous identity, voice, and contextual methods while sharing deep relational belonging, regional pastoral care, and mutual accountability (`Global coordinates. Regional contextualizes. Local leads.`).
- **Health above results inside pioneer work:** Pioneer purpose toward unreached places is paired explicitly with long-term regional care for spiritual, emotional, physical, financial, and family health, inclusive of single workers and candid about struggle and rest.
- **Reciprocal fellowship over transactional charity:** Concrete practices such as The Bridge Project (explicitly requiring no financial giving), listening-first Exposure Trips where local hosts set terms, and local-leader storytelling invite every participant to both give and receive.
- **Lean stewardship grounded in shared dependence:** Support-raised staff and transparent financial reporting keep institutional overhead lean while naming the separately reported 2025 Home Office funding lines (`$192.5k` 8% admin fee with entity/fund scope pending Finance review under `OVR-01`, `$40.2k` Home Office & fee-coverage gifts, `$33k` rent, and `$14.5k` WMN Inc.) openly beside designated field giving ([Open Verification Register](open-verification-register.md)).
