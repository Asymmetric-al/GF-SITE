**Section Label:** `OUR THREE PILLARS`

1. **Church Planting:** Proclaiming Christ's death and resurrection, making disciples, baptizing believers, and gathering healthy, multiplying local churches.
2. **Local Leadership:** Empowering believers who already belong to the language, culture, and community to shepherd and multiply the church.

## Block `COPY-CORE-03`: Founding Story ("Who Leads When a Missionary Arrives?")

**Headline:** **Before we started Global Fellowship, we learned to follow a local leader.**  
**Body:**  
In Liberia, before founding Global Fellowship in Auburn, California in 1989, Don and Kathy Oates served under the leadership of a local church leader. That relationship marked the organization for good. Local believers did not need an outside headquarters to run their ministry; they needed brothers and sisters willing to stand beside them in prayer, care, friendship, and practical help.

When Global Fellowship expanded into cross-cultural sending in 2012, uniting near-culture, South-to-South, and North American workers, that order did not change. When a cross-cultural missionary arrives in a new neighborhood today, the local leader still leads the work.

## Block `COPY-CORE-04`: How the Fellowship Works (Three Connected Levels)

**Headline:** **Global coordinates. Regional contextualizes. Local leads.**  
**Body:**  
Global Fellowship is one worldwide family made up of local church planters, regional care teams, cross-cultural missionaries, sending churches, and support staff:

- **Global staff remove barriers:** Home Office and international support teams raise personal support alongside field workers, handling administration, donor care, open financial reporting, and church connections so church planters stay free to shepherd people.
- **Regional teams contextualize care:** Leaders living in the region (including our Southeast Asia hub in Chiang Mai, opened in 2024) adapt training, legal safety, and personal care for singles, couples, children, and teenagers under _Health Above Results_.
- **Local leaders lead ministry in context:** They know their neighbors, choose methods fitted to their community, and keep their own ministry name, voice, and leadership without being absorbed into an outside template.
