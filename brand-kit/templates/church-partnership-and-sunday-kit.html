CHURCH PARTNERSHIP & EXPOSURE TRIP BRIEF

Who decides whether a visit would help? The local church hosting it can say no

Where a neighborhood has no local church in its own language, Global Fellowship unites indigenous church planters and cross-cultural missionaries in Christ across three pillars (Church Planting, Local Leadership, Unreached People) so partner churches share multi-year prayer, sending, and listening-first friendship.

OFFICIAL VERBATIM TEXT · Our Mission

“We make disciples and plant new churches by empowering local leaders and sending cross-cultural missionaries to pioneer church-planting work among unreached people.”

Listening-First Exposure Trips: Two-Sided Agreement

Before any flights are booked, both congregations speak candidly. “Not this year” is always a respected decision from a local host.

Side A: What the Local Host Decides

  • Season & timing: Whether welcoming guests strengthens or strains local households right now, including full freedom to decline or postpone.
  • Purpose & team size: Exact headcount, focus, and daily rhythm shaped around normal church life.
  • Cameras & sharing: Clear boundaries on phones, photos, names, and social media before anyone lands.
  • Teaching & hospitality: Local believers set the agenda so visiting churches arrive as learners.

Side B: What the Visiting Church Commits

  • Listen before advising: Leave outside agendas and uninvited project plans at home.
  • Honor local safety rules: Obey every photo, name, and livestream boundary on site and back home.
  • Stay beyond the flight home: Pray regularly, correspond through The Bridge Project, and serve alongside the regional team over years.
  • Inspect open books: Review Global Fellowship’s Statements of Activity and Candid profile (8043461) freely with your elders.

Month 1 · Elder Review

1. Two-Minute Spoken Pulpit Script (Safe for Livestreams)

“Good morning, church. Before we pray together, picture a small living room this morning where neighbors are opening the Gospel of Luke in their own mother tongue, led by a pastor from their own community. Through Global Fellowship, our church gets to stand alongside brothers and sisters like them: indigenous church planters and cross-cultural teammates united in one family in Christ, where local believers lead the work in their own neighborhoods and missionaries serve alongside and under them.

You will find a prayer insert in your bulletin today. Because some of our brothers and sisters serve in places where public names could bring real harm, we do not put every name or city on our livestream. God knows them by name, and we can pray faithfully at home. One simple way your household or small group can begin this week is The Bridge Project: you exchange letters and pray with a local church planter, with no financial gift expected. Let us pray together right now.”

Optional Speaker Footnote (for elder Q&A): Global Fellowship was founded in 1989, expanded into North American cross-cultural sending in 2012, and opened its Chiang Mai Southeast Asia hub in 2024 as part of a polycentric church-planting movement where majority-world churches increasingly send across urban and near-culture corridors. Roughly 3.6 billion people live in unreached people groups (UPGs: 2% or fewer evangelical Christians and 5% or fewer Christian adherents), including unengaged, unreached people groups (UUPGs) with no known active church-planting strategy (Joshua Project, consulted November 2026; exact snapshot pending confirmation before public printing).
