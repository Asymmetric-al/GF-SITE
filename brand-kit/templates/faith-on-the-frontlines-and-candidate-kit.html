Coffee, honest questions, and a visiting church planter

Host Run-of-Show (75 Minutes)

  1. Welcome & Coffee (15 min): Ordinary hospitality around food; no formal stage or fundraising appeal.
  2. Firsthand Witness (25 min): The visiting church planter chooses the emphasis and boundaries (confirm phone, photo, and recording rules before starting).
  3. Open Q&A & Reciprocal Prayer (20 min): Invite the visiting planter, if they wish, to ask how their congregation can pray for your neighborhood too.
  4. Prayer & Side-Table Options (15 min): Pray together; place prayer cards, Bridge Project sign-up, and open financial statements on a side table without passing a plate.

Pressure-Free Host Closing Script

“Thank you for welcoming our friend tonight. If you want to stay connected, three simple options sit on the table: take a prayer card home, join The Bridge Project to exchange letters with no financial ask, or read our open financial statements to partner financially. Whatever you choose, thank you for praying with us.”

Host preparation (in-person or remote): Confirm visiting planter availability, security tier, and agenda preferences with Global Engagement at info@globalfellowship.org before inviting guests. Regional hubs and remote churches outside Northern California can host video conversations using the same hospitality-first format.

No Fundraising Pitch

Guests are never put on the spot with a basket, pledge card, or countdown appeal; giving stays strictly voluntary.

Local Voice First

The visiting church planter shapes what is shared and decides whether photos or recordings are appropriate.

Take-Home Open Books

OFFICIAL VERBATIM TEXT · Our Mission & Three Pillars

“We make disciples and plant new churches by empowering local leaders and sending cross-cultural missionaries to pioneer church-planting work among unreached people.”

Three Pillars: Church Planting · Local Leadership · Unreached People

1. Who leads when I arrive?

The local leader still leads. Rooted in service under a local pastor in Liberia before 1989 (cross-cultural sending began in 2012), missionaries serve as learners under local elders.

2. Funding, debt, and global sending paths?

Our regional hub team (including Chiang Mai) walks candidly through whether paid local work is legally permitted, how sending-church support teams work in your home currency, and realistic debt plans before you set any dates.

3. How does discernment involve my church?

Bring your home pastor or elders early, whether sending from Asia, Africa, Latin America, Europe, or North America: listening call, doctrinal review, regional fit, and language prep paced to host readiness.

4. Who cares for my soul, mind, and household?

Regional directors guard spiritual, emotional, physical, and financial health under Health Above Results for single workers, couples, kids, and teens, with confidential counseling referrals.

5. What happens when conflict arises?

Regional leads mediate early, and every worker has direct access to our International Member Care Facilitator outside their reporting line whenever independent care is needed.

6. What happens after my first message?

A sending or regional teammate replies directly to schedule a calm conversation and read our historic evangelical Statement of Faith together (8 public articles plus full doctrinal review).
